# Changelog

All notable changes to Mealwright are listed here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Version numbers follow the plan in `docs/spec.md` section 8: 0.N.0 per build milestone (not published), then MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH from 1.0.0.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **Cooking ahead (0.8.0, F159, cabledrawer 6 Nov).** Cooked it works on a meal planned for a later day (Plan → Cook → Start cooking → Confirm); the meal stays on its day. Stock is taken when it is really cooked, and a tub's use-by counts from the day it was really cooked (a Saturday batch cooked on Friday counts from Friday). Didn't is still refused before the meal's day; Move it is unchanged. No database change.
- **Today: a meal cooked in parts (0.8.0, F160).** One line per recipe: a tick and a **Cooked** chip on each cooked one, a filled **Cooked it** button on each one still to cook; the header says "1 of 2 cooked", then "All cooked". It replaces 0.7's "Cooked <recipe>" line. A recipe removed from the recipe list shows as a plain line, without Cooked it.
- **Plan: a meal cooked in parts (0.8.0, F162, F163).** The panel lists each recipe with a **Cooked** tag on cooked ones, and the note names them ("Banana bread was cooked. Undo its Cooked it to move or remove it."); a recipe not yet cooked can be removed or have its servings changed on its own. Move it names only what moves ("Moved Lentil soup from Dinner on Thu 5 Nov to Dinner on Sat 7 Nov").
- **Cook view's day header (0.8.0, F164).** "N things to cook" counts only what is not cooked yet; when everything is cooked it says **All cooked**.
- **Small words (0.8.0, F133).** "Add 2 eggs to the shopping list"; "How much is 1 sprig of curry leaves?"; a Cook once plan is logged as "Planned Sambar once for 3 tubs"; "Did you cook …?" lists every recipe and tub in the meal.
- **Plan Month: Both · Eat · Cook (0.8.0, F149, cabledrawer 6 Nov).** The switch shows on the Month too and is the same remembered choice as the Week's. Phone Month: Eat shows meal dots and the day's meals; Cook a blue ring on cooking days and the day's cooking with **Start cooking**; Both rings and dots and both lists, with a line under the grid saying what the marks mean. Computer Month: Cook shows only the cooking chips (blue, pan); Eat and Both as before.
- **Today's This week (0.8.0, F150).** Seven rows from today (Today first): the day, the dinner's name (or the day's last meal), "3 meals" or "tub · 1 meal", and a blue **Cook** mark on a day with cooking; an empty day says **Nothing planned** with **+ Plan**, which opens the Plan on that day's week. Rows are at least 44 px and long names end with "…"; the same rows on the phone and the computer.
- **The Plan's add sheet says what to do first (0.8.0, F127).** "Pick a meal, then what to add" over the day; after picking, "Add to" over "Dinner · Wednesday 11".

### Fixed

- **The first toast after Confirm (0.8.0, F161)** names only the recipes that press cooked.
- **The toast with Undo after Confirm in Cook mode (0.8.0, F158)** stays its full time on the screen Cook mode returns to. A lost reply to Confirm no longer leaves an all-cooked meal "planned": the next load of Today or the Plan shows it cooked (once the press is a few minutes old), and Undo of it puts the meal back to planned. A recipe deleted from the recipe list is left out of the Cooked it review.
- **Cook mode's labels (0.8.0)** no longer change for a moment while the plan loads.
- **Week meal names (0.8.0, F137)** break only between words, end with "…" when too long, and show in full on hover and focus.
- **Undo of a plan change that could only put back part (0.8.0, F140)** names the meal: "Put back what could be for Banana bread (Sun breakfast): some of it has changed again since." Other Undos keep their words.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-11-06

The cooking milestone: "Cooked it" takes what a meal used from the pantry, with a review first; Cook mode walks through a recipe step by step; the Plan gets Both · Eat · Cook, COOK and EAT cards, portions and a Month view with "Fill from freezer first"; staples show when they were last restocked; and many import, shopping and pantry fixes from cabledrawer's hand tests. Words not yet confirmed by cabledrawer are marked "proposed, for cabledrawer" in `docs/dev/words.md`.

**Before you install it: back up your data.** This version changes the database (new tables and columns for cooked records, ingredient links on method steps, who eats each meal, notes on the plan, cooking per step, and a staple's restock date and when it was typed; nothing existing is rewritten), and the old version can't read a database the new one has changed. The steps are the same as before (`docs/dev/test-on-homelab.md` section 4.1: stop Mealwright, then back up the data folder). From this version Mealwright also makes its own safety copy of the database before every upgrade (see Added, K1), but that copy holds only the database, not the photos, so keep making the backup.

### Added

- **Import Check: change or remove a method step (0.7.0, F111).** On the Check screen a step or a heading is a button: tap it to change its words in a box (Done, Cancel; Enter is Done, Escape is Cancel; an empty one can't be kept and offers Remove). Each step and heading has Remove, with a toast and Undo that puts it back in its place (also after a Split into shorter steps); the step numbers follow, and headings take none. Nothing is saved until Save, which sends the edited method as it already sent the method (no server change); a draft keeps the edits. The box and the code behind it load when a step is first tapped, so the page does not grow. Words are in `docs/dev/words.md` section 35, proposed, for cabledrawer. Unit and browser tests (phone, computer, WebKit).
- **Paper cuts: the recipe editor carries on where you left off (0.7.0, PC1).** New recipe and Edit recipe keep what you have typed in the tab (sessionStorage, one for each recipe, never sent anywhere). Reload or close and reopen the editor and it says "Carrying on where you left off" with a **Start over** link, which drops it and opens the saved recipe again. Save, Cancel and Delete clear it; a recipe someone else saved meanwhile meets the usual "changed this recipe" note on Save rather than being written over. A photo still waiting to be saved is not kept, only a photo taken off. Works without browser storage. Browser, unit and editor tests.
- **Duplicate on the recipe page (0.7.0, PC1).** The recipe page's ⋯ menu has **Duplicate**: it makes "Sambar (copy)" and opens it in the editor. The copy has everything in the recipe (lines, steps and their ingredient links, tags, nutrition, Serves, Makes, Made ahead, where it came from) and nothing of what happened to the original (cooked history, the plan). The photo is copied to files of its own (proposed, for cabledrawer), so removing it from one never touches the other. Made ahead recipes: the copy gets its own pantry ingredient named like the copy, and a name already used gets "(copy 2)", "(copy 3)" and so on (proposed, for cabledrawer). New API call `POST /api/v1/recipes/{id}/duplicate` (docs/dev/api-0.4-recipes.md); no database change. Backend, unit and browser tests.
- **Import Check: Made in this recipe (0.7 alpha 2, F108).** A line made earlier in the same recipe ("2 teaspoons spice mix (from the spice blend)") can be set to Made in this recipe: it is saved as words only, never tied to an ingredient, never taken from the pantry, and not counted in "will be saved without an ingredient". The choice comes first when the note says "from the …" or the name is one of the recipe's own section headings. A draft keeps it (a new kind of choice, "here", in the drafts API).
- **Import Check: Remove line (0.7 alpha 2, F105).** Each ingredient line has a Remove line button. The line is not saved, not counted (the header counts and "N ingredients") and the toast has Undo, which puts it back in its place with its choice.
- **Import Check: "water and broth" with one amount is two lines, and Split into two (0.7 alpha 2, F109).** "4 1/2 cups water and broth (see instructions)" is Water and Broth, each with no amount and the note "4½ cups together". Any ingredient line has Split into two for the cases the reader misses (with Undo). A name with "and", "&" or "plus" in it is never learned as another name of an ingredient.
- **Ingredients: add an ingredient straight from the search box (0.7 alpha 2, F97).** Typing a name that is not exactly the name, or another name, of an ingredient shows "+ New ingredient: 'chilli flakes'" at the end of the results, also beside partial matches. Tapping it (or pressing Enter when nothing was found) opens the usual "How do you count it?" question with the name filled in, then opens the new ingredient. It is hidden while the server can't be reached.
- **"+ Place": type a new place wherever a place is picked (0.7.0, F101).** The last choice of every place picker is now "+ Place" (Add to pantry, the ingredient's "Lives in", a recipe import line's "Usually kept in", Shopping's Put away panel and a tick's "Change", a packet's "Move to"). It asks for a name and "Kept cold?" (Cupboard · Fridge · Freezer), makes the place last in the order and picks it; a name the household already has just picks that place. New API call `POST /api/v1/places` (`kind` fridge, freezer or cupboard drives "freezer last"; a repeated name is `409 place_exists` carrying that place; not logged as a change, like "+ Shop"; no database change). A new place is not queued: offline, "+ Place" says it needs the server, while picking an existing place offline works as before. Renaming, reordering and deleting places stay in Settings (0.8). A test shows an ingredient in two places is one total for a recipe's "in the pantry", for what Shopping buys and for Running low (it already was). The words in the box are proposed, for cabledrawer. A household keeps at most 50 places (`409 too_many_places`, with a plain reason the box shows; the cap: cabledrawer 1 Nov). Other phones' pickers fetch the places again when the ingredient editor or Check opens; a live update no longer closes the box in Put away; Escape closes only the box. Backend, unit and browser tests.
- **Importer consistency, the reader (0.7.0, C1).** The same thing written two ways now gives the same result. Amounts: "3-or-so cups", "one and a half cups", "a handful" and "Handful dried banana chips" (a loose size word, no amount), amounts after the name ("garlic, 2 cloves, minced", "olive oil - 2 tbsp", "Flour 500g"), "1 14-ounce can" (a pack multiple), "heaped" and "level" before a unit, quarts, gallons and fluid ounces (read with the household's pint), "a dozen eggs" as 12 and "2 cloves garlic" as 2 when the ingredient has no such size. Names: footnote marks dropped ("(Note 5)", "(, full fat)"), "heavy / thickened cream" and "black or white pepper" share their noun, "skinless, boneless chicken" keeps its name past the comma, a variety in brackets joins the name ("Kashmiri dried red chilies"), a colour in brackets is a note and never a "Same as" (and matching ignores colour and size words alone), a prep word shows once, "made with …" and "and cut into chunks" go to the note. Lines: "or" gives the first choice and a note; "a + b" and "a and b to taste" give two lines; "For the Lamb", "For the Garnish:" and "To assemble" are headings in both lists and take no step number; lines under "For serving" or "To garnish" get the note "optional"; the page's buttons ("Cook Mode", "½x1×2×") are left out; "cuts into 10 slices" fills Serves; the US-pints banner shows only when a line says pints; several links pasted are read one at a time (the first; `more_links` says how many were left) and never become a recipe named after an address; an "ABBREVIATED RECIPE" that repeats a "FULL RECIPE" is left out. New ingredients: milk, cream, raw meat and the like are kept in the Fridge. Converted amounts are rounded as a cook writes them (340 g; two decimals only below 10 g or ml). Recipe names lose a trailing "recipe", "How to Make …" and "(Authentic … Recipe)"; tags get capitals and a small table of synonyms; a new ingredient's name gets tidy capitals on save. Add an item reads "2 packets milk". No new library, no database change, no new outgoing connection. "Chicken bouillon" and "corned beef" are kept in the Cupboard and fish fingers in the Freezer.
- **Import Check screen, consistent (0.7.0, C2).** Each ingredient line on Check is its own card with a status dot (Ready, To confirm, Needs you, Staple; the dot has its meaning in words for screen readers). A line that is not in your ingredients shows the name it read before the amount ("milk · 2 tbsp · not in your ingredients", F5). The amount and the note are chips: tap one to fix the amount, unit and note, with Done (F30.6); a draft keeps the fix. Every line that needs a choice has **Treat as staple**, also as a switch in the New ingredient form (F21); the form closes with Done and shows as a settled line with Edit (F10); an ingredient made or picked on one line is used on lines of the same import that read as the same name, with "Also used for 1 more line" (F6). Save with lines that have no ingredient opens a sheet that goes through them one at a time (New ingredient, Pick another, Treat as staple, Skip this line), with Back to Check and Save anyway (F4). Long method steps (four sentences or more) get **Split into shorter steps**: a preview first, then Keep the split or Keep as one, and Put the steps back as they were (F13). A recipe photo, on the recipe page and on Check, opens full size; Esc, a tap or Close closes it and focus goes back to the photo (F29). No server change.
- **Cooking data (0.7, K1; no screens yet).** A **safety copy before every upgrade**: when Mealwright starts and the database needs upgrading, it first copies it to `data/backups/pre-upgrade-<revision>-<date>.db` (UTC) and, once the upgrade has worked, keeps the last three such copies (while an upgrade keeps failing it makes no new copy and deletes none, and the copy from before the first try, named in `data/backups/upgrade-failed`, is kept); no copy on a new install or when there is nothing to upgrade, and if the copy fails Mealwright doesn't upgrade or start, and the log says why (`docs/dev/test-on-homelab.md` also now says to stop the container before copying the data folder by hand). A **staple's restock date**: ticking a staple on Shopping ("Restocked") moves it on; unticking or Undo puts it back; read-only as `restocked_at` on `GET /foods` and `GET /foods/{id}` (null when not a staple or never restocked); merging two ingredients keeps the newer of their stored dates (a ticked line moves across with its tick), and Undo puts it back. **Step ↔ ingredient links** for Cook mode: each method step can list the ingredient lines it uses, each marked `guessed` or `by_hand`; read with the recipe (`steps[].lines`), set by hand in the recipe save (`steps[].lines`, positions in the save's `lines`); a save that leaves them out keeps a step's links while its words are unchanged and the line is still there. The **cooked record**: one per meal item or batch cook, with a snapshot of the recipe as cooked (name, version, servings, lines with amounts, sizes and ingredients, steps), the portions cooked and the change holding its stock changes; it outlives later edits and the recipe being deleted for good. Read-only: `GET /plan/items/{plan_item_id}/cooked`, `GET /plan/batches/{plan_batch_id}/cooked`; "Cooked it" writes it later in 0.7. Database: three new migrations (`b89c3c1918f8` restock date, `31a5838bc5a2` step links, `6612736d5aae` cooked records); going back drops only the new data.
- **Cooking: the deduction engine (0.7, K2; no screens yet).** `POST /plan/items/{id}/cook/preview` and `POST /plan/batches/{id}/cook/preview` work out what "Cooked it" would take from the pantry, one row per ingredient: scaled to the portions cooked, converted only when the units differ (the household's answers over the bundled table, an unknown conversion asked once), the packet that goes off first, fridge and cupboard before the freezer (the freezer only on request), staples never taken, never below zero (the shortfall comes back for "add to list"), "Can use instead" ingredients taken from when one has none, and a recipe used as an ingredient taken from the pantry or offered as "Make it first?". `POST /plan/items/{id}/cook` and `POST /plan/batches/{id}/cook` confirm the person's choices (Use on or off, Used or Left typed, the freezer): one change in Recent changes with a line for each packet and one Undo, and the cooked record keeps each row as confirmed (`snapshot.choices`, no database change). A second confirm for the same item or batch is refused, and a batch takes its ingredients once. A whole meal with several recipes is one confirm and one Undo (`POST /plan/meals/{id}/cook` and `/cook/preview`; the recipes share one pool of packets). "Add to list" is its own tick, apart from Use (a row with nothing in the house offers its shortfall ticked; freezer-only stock and "running low", where what is left is at or below the ingredient's running-low level, offer it unticked), and Undo gives the list back as it was only where nobody has changed it since. Eating a tub (`POST /plan/tubs/{id}/eat`) uses the tub, a whole one by default or in quarters, and never the ingredients. What is missing goes on the shopping list when confirmed (ticked to start), a "Can use instead" ingredient is used only when the ingredient has none at all, stock only in the freezer starts switched off, the opened packet goes first among equal use-by dates and a packet that would be left under 2 % is taken whole. Also: the shopping list's counter is written the moment a number is taken (a follow-up to 0.6). New words are marked "proposed, for cabledrawer" in `docs/dev/words.md` section 27.
- **"Cooked it", the review (0.7.0, K3).** Today's "Did you cook…?" card, a meal eaten today that is still planned, and the Plan's menu for a meal whose day has come all open one review of what comes out of the pantry (table: Ingredient · From · Before → after · Take · Use, one Use switch on every row, Used / Left with a stepper for counted ingredients and a box for g and ml). **Portions cooked − N +** at the top asks again and every row follows. A meal with several recipes has one tab for each recipe and one Confirm; Confirm takes it all as one change with one Undo, which also takes back the meal's answer (Undo gives back what the cook took even after later changes, never below zero, "Put back what could be" when it can't all come back, for 7 days). "Any left over?" and the meal's answer stay part of the same flow. An ingredient with nothing in the pantry starts off with **Bought some — used them**, which changes no stock and is kept on the cooked record (new choice `bought`, shown in `snapshot.choices`). A Cook once batch is cooked from its cook day's menu, once, for the cook-day eaters and its tubs; a batch that was cooked can't be taken off the plan ("This was cooked on … Undo “Cooked it” first."). **Recipe and pantry in different units (F106):** each side keeps its own unit; the same unit is taken off as it is; with a learned link the amount is proposed in the pantry's unit and marked **estimate**, and can be changed; with nothing learned the review asks in the pantry's unit (grams or ml, **About half** of the first packet, **Skip**) and learns the answer ("Remember for next time" on; a new answer replaces the old one); it never blocks Confirm, and works for any pair of units that one number can settle (cups against a count asks the old question). **"2 cloves garlic" (F89):** a line with no amount and "2 cloves" or "3 sprigs" in its note asks the same way and learns the ingredient's size "clove". One ingredient in several places (fridge and cupboard) is one total on its row, as on Shopping (F101). API additions (docs/dev/api-0.2.md 6.3d): a row's `estimate`, `unit`, `half`, `ask_need`, `count_note` and its lines' `amount` and `unit`; the choice `bought`; up to 20 `answers` instead of 5; a size answer replaces an earlier size of that name only inside a cook, for the size a "2 cloves" note asked about (its Undo puts it back); `DELETE /plan/batches/{id}` of a cooked batch is `409`. No database change, no new library, no new outgoing connection.
- **Portions, Serves and Notes on the Plan (0.7.0, P1).** A batch now makes a fixed number of portions: its tubs plus the cook-day meal. **"Leftovers on…"** (in a recipe's menu) lists the meals after the cooking and up to the use-by with a tick each, so several can be planned at once, and shows the portions left ("Makes 6 · 2 eaten Sun · 3 tubs planned · 2 picked, only 1 left"). When none are left it says **"No portions left"** and offers **Cook more** (a bigger batch; amounts and the shopping list follow) or **Add anyway** (the meal is planned for one serving; the batch stays the same). **Serves − N +** in the meal's menu changes how many a recipe is for (amounts and the list follow), and works on meals planned before this version (no number was set: it starts from the recipe's own Serves). **Who's eating** is ticked per meal, by sign-in until member profiles in 0.8 (nobody ticked means everyone; proposed, for cabledrawer). **+ Note** (in + Add) puts "Eating out", "Takeaway" or "At Amma's" in a meal: it counts as a filled meal, needs nothing bought, is never asked "Did you cook?", and Cook once and Fill from freezer first leave it alone. New API: `GET /plan/items/{id}/portions`, `POST /plan/items/{id}/leftovers`, `PATCH /plan/items/{id}` (servings), `PUT /plan/meals/{id}/eaters`, `note` on `POST /plan/items`, and `portions`, `portions_left`, `eating`, `eater_login_ids` and `note` in `GET /plan` (docs/dev/api-0.2.md). **Database:** two migrations (a `note` kind of meal item; a `meal_eater` table); going back removes only the notes and who was ticked. The drag zone "Leftovers here" and the one-day picker ask the same way ("No portions left": Cook more or Add anyway), and a spare tub is never planned for a day after its use-by. The plan stays online-only.
- **Cook mode (0.7.0, K4).** **Cook now**, a main button above the ingredients on the recipe page, opens a full-screen Cook mode: big text, one step at a time, with **Back** and **Next** (the arrow keys and a sideways swipe do the same), and a bar showing how far you are ("Step 2 of 4"). A heading in the method is a section title above its steps and is never counted as a step; a recipe with two method sections shows every step in order, each under its own heading, and a recipe with no steps, one step or one very long step does not break it. **Show ingredients** slides up the whole list on every step. A time in a step's words ("10 minutes", "1 h", "1 hour 30 minutes", "10-15 minutes") gets a **Start timer** button; the timer counts down on the screen, the same timer is kept in view on the other steps, and it ends with one short, gentle chime, made in code with the browser's own Web Audio and played three times (no sound file, no choice of sound yet). Cook mode says there is no alarm if the phone is locked or the app is in the background. **Keep screen awake** moves from the recipe page into Cook mode, where it turns on by itself; where the browser can't do it the switch is greyed with "This browser can’t keep the screen on". **Ingredients for each step** is an Experimental option (a small pink flask): off by default, it shows under each step the ingredients the app guesses belong to it (the step's words include the ingredient's name, its plural or one of its other names, or the links the recipe already holds), labelled "Experimental · guessed". Leaving with a timer running asks "Stop the timer and leave?" (Stay · Leave), also for the browser's Back. The last step ends with **← Back** and **Cooked it** (into the review); the X at the top left leaves (F155, F156). **Cook now plans first (cabledrawer 1 Nov):** it plans the recipe into today's next meal (the order cabledrawer accepted on 1 Nov: the meal today that already has it and isn't cooked yet, else the first meal slot today with no meal yet, else the first meal still planned; if every meal is cooked, nothing is planned), says where in a notice at the top of Cook mode ("Planned for today's Dinner") with **Undo**, and then **Cooked it** works and the pantry stays right; if the recipe is already planned today nothing is added; after Undo, or when the plan can't be written (the notice says "This recipe wasn’t planned."), only **← Back** is shown on the last step (F156). **Ingredients for each step is a household setting (cabledrawer 1 Nov):** off by default, and once switched on it stays on for the household; it is read and saved on the server (a new `cook_per_step` setting, one small database change that adds a column and goes back cleanly), another phone sees the change the next time it opens Cook mode, and a device that had the old per-device switch on does not switch the household on. Any signed-in person of the household may change this one switch (cabledrawer 1 Nov), while the pint stays for admins only. When the recipe is already planned today in a meal with other recipes, **Cooked it** reviews only that recipe (cabledrawer 1 Nov). If the switch can't be saved it goes back and says so. Cook mode shows the recipe's own amounts. No new library; nothing is sent anywhere; the content security policy is unchanged.

Cooking is blue with a pan icon (new colour tokens, checked for contrast in both themes). New words are in `docs/dev/words.md` section 31, proposed, for cabledrawer.

- **Restock dates (0.7.0, K5).** A staple's chip on Pantry has a second line, "Restocked 12 Oct" ("Restocked today" on the day); the Ingredients column says "Restocked 3 weeks ago" under Staple, with the whole date on hover and on tap (on the phone the row's quiet line has the words and the editor has the date). ### Security

The security passes on each 0.6 pull request found these before release; none was in 0.5.
- **The shopping list's counter:** an Undo of a plan change inside another write could wind the list's change number back, after which every list write failed. One transaction now never reuses or lowers a number (tested).
- **Shopping from the plan:** a recipe whose amounts are too big to add up gives that line no amount instead of a slow or failing sum, in any order; each recipe is worked out once per update; "Got it" takes the household's lock.

  - **The review** ("Check before saving") shows every ingredient line as written, how it was read, and what it is in your kitchen: Matched, To confirm ("Same as Onions" or New ingredient), Needs you (Pick an ingredient, New ingredient or Treat as staple) or Staple, with the counts, the photo ("Photo not downloaded" while "Download recipe photos" is off; otherwise the main photo is downloaded and waits until you save), and what was left out ("4 lines after “Tips” were left out", a page with more than 200 lines or 100 steps, steps cut to 5,000 characters). The name, Serves and minutes can be changed there. Lines you don't choose for are saved with their words only. Save & plan it is hidden until 0.6.
  - **Save** makes the recipe, the new ingredients, the new staples and the other names it learned ("Same as Onions" for "red onion" adds "red onion" as another name for Onions) in one go, and puts **one** entry in Ingredients → Recent changes whose Undo takes all of it back. An imported recipe keeps its web address, the import date and the author, and its page says "From: bbcgoodfood.com · by Meena". The recipe editor's Source box has its hint back: "A web address shows as a link you can open yourself. Mealwright never visits it."
  - **A reading line says when it picked one amount from several** (a range, two units, a pack multiple): the saved line keeps the line as written in its note.
  - **The database** gets three new, optional columns on `recipe` (the address, the import date and the author); every recipe already there has none. One migration, tested up and down. How it works: `docs/dev/recipe-import.md`, "The import's API".
- **Ounces and pounds count (cabledrawer 23 Oct).** "8 oz spinach" is 226.8 g and "1 lb potatoes" is 453.6 g (1 oz = 28.35 g, 1 lb = 453.6 g); pints and fluid ounces stay in the note. "3–2 tbsp" is 3 tbsp (the higher number), and "2 x 2 x 400g" is 1,600 g.
- **At most two recipe pages are read at once** for the whole server (cabledrawer 23 Oct); a third waits its turn, within the import's 30 seconds, and otherwise says "Mealwright is busy reading another page. Try again in a moment."
- **A real-recipe check script (0.5 PR 6; dev only, not run yet).** `backend/scripts/import_check.py` fetches the recipe pages on cabledrawer's list one at a time (at most one request every 5 seconds per site, with the app's own fetching rules), reads them, and writes a report of what the reader found. It downloads no photos, saves no pages, and is not part of `./check` or the Docker image. How to run it: `docs/dev/recipe-import.md`.

- **Recipe import, behind the scenes (0.5 PR 2).** Three pieces of the server that the Import screens will use. Nothing shows on a screen yet, nothing goes online, and the database is unchanged.
  - **An ingredient line reader.** It turns a line such as "2 onions, finely chopped" into an amount, a unit, a name and a note. It reads every number form (½, 1½, 1 1/2, 1/2, 0.5, 1,5), takes the higher number of a range, takes the metric amount when a line gives two units (a cup alone stays a cup), reads UK packets ("1 x 400g tin chopped tomatoes"), and keeps size words ("1 sprig", "a handful") as lines that count only if the ingredient has that size. Lines with no amount, such as "salt to taste", never count. A line it can't read is a result, not an error.
  - **Matching against your ingredients.** Each line is Matched (by name or another name, ignoring case, plurals and prep words), To confirm (a close match such as "red onion" for Onions), Needs you, or Staple. A size word counts only when the ingredient has that size, and a cup is your household's own cup. It also works out the first guess for a new ingredient. Nothing is written until Save.
  - **Pasted recipe text.** It finds the title, ingredients and method from common headings (or by the look of the lines), strips hidden characters, keeps at most 200 lines and 100 steps, and refuses more than 100,000 characters.
  - Notes for developers are in `docs/dev/ingredient-reader.md`.

- **Recipe import, the server's first half** (no screens yet; they come later in 0.5). Mealwright can now fetch a recipe page from a link and read the recipe out of it, and download the recipe's main photo. Nothing is fetched while the Privacy switches are off: "Recipe import from links" for the page and "Download recipe photos" for the photo, which both start off. Every link, and every place it sends you on to (at most 5), is checked before anything is sent: web links only, ports 80 and 443 only, and public addresses only. The request goes to exactly the address that was checked, so a site can't pass the check and then point somewhere private. A slow site is cut off after 15 seconds, and a whole fetch, redirects included, after 30. Pages up to 5 MB, web pages only; photos up to 15 MB and 50 megapixels, then shrunk and cleaned like an uploaded photo (hidden details such as the place it was taken are removed). Log lines never say which site was visited. How it works: `docs/dev/recipe-import.md`.
- **This server itself, link-local and cloud "metadata" addresses are never visited**, whatever the settings.
- **The "Your own network" list, on the server** (the screen comes later in 0.5): addresses on your home network (your router, a home wiki) are refused unless an admin puts their name, address or range on this list, which also opens other ports for them. It starts empty, belongs to the household like the Privacy switches, and refuses anything always blocked, ranges that are too wide, and more than 100 entries. This adds one database table; nothing existing is rewritten.
- **The page reader** reads the recipe data most recipe sites include for search engines (schema.org JSON-LD, and microdata on older pages), with Python's own tools and no new libraries: the name, a short description (cut at 200 characters), total time, Serves or Makes, nutrition per serving, the ingredients and method with their section names as headings, the category and cuisine as tags (never the page's keywords), the main photo and the author. Up to 200 ingredient lines and 100 steps are kept, and the import knows when a page had more. Hidden characters are taken out of the page's text; Tamil and Hindi names keep the joiners they need. Tested on made-up pages shaped like eight kinds of recipe site; no real page is stored in the repo. A page that tries to make the reader slow or greedy (very long numbers, thousands of tiny JSON objects, and the like) is cut short, and as a safety net for anything not yet thought of, each page is read in a separate short-lived process with a time limit and a memory limit (384 MB): past either, the import says "Couldn’t read that page. Paste the recipe’s text instead." (The time limit is now counted in the child's own processor time, 12 seconds, with a 15-second clock backstop; see the 0.5 PR 3 entry.)

### Changed

- **The time limit for reading a page** counts the reading process's own processor time (12 seconds) with a 15-second clock backstop, instead of 5 seconds of clock: the realistic big test page needs about 1.2 seconds of processor time on a fast PC, perhaps 5 to 6 seconds on a Raspberry Pi 4 class board (an estimate), which the old limit could have refused. The reading process also can't write a core file, and it switches on the network guard itself (it starts fresh, so it doesn't have the server's). The guard now lives in its own small module (`network_guard.py`), so the reading process starts in 0.1 second instead of 0.9. A result that can't be sent back, or a reading process that dies, is "crash" in the log, not "memory", and a process that answered but doesn't end is killed after 2 seconds.

### Fixed

- **The name look-up now counts towards the import's 30 seconds (spec section 6, question 40).** Finding a site's address from its name could run past the 30 seconds if the computer's name service was slow; it now waits at most the time the import has left, and then says "That site took too long to answer. Try again later." This covers the link, each place it sends you on to, and the photo. Anything else that looks up a name through the outbound gate waits at most 15 seconds. No new connection is made and the database is unchanged.
- A page whose total time was a superscript or circled digit ("²", "①") raised an error instead of being read as no time.
- A control character between words (U+001C to U+001F) joined them, and private-use or unassigned characters stayed in an ingredient line; a cut no longer splits a country flag or a skin tone, and a text of only combining marks is cut at the limit, not to nothing. An amount that converts to more than 1,000,000 g or ml no longer counts.
- The real-recipe check script stopped before reading any page when run on its own: an import loop that the tests hid, because they load the app first. Its first real run (24 Oct) fetched every page but read none. A test now starts the script in a fresh Python, both as a person runs it and as the page reader's own process loads it.
- **Shopping, "Different pack?" → Other…** no longer sends 1004.9999999999999 for 1.005 kg, which the server refused; it sends 1005.
- **Security (0.5 PR 7):** a photo file that can't be deleted (the hourly sweep, an upload's clean-up, or the clean-up inside writing the files) logs only the kind of error, never the file's path; Undo's remaining restore steps (`_restore_field`, `_name`, `_size`, `_stand_in`, `_conversion`, `_lot`) check the household themselves, with a two-household test for each; "Your own network" refuses `localhost.localdomain`, `ip6-localhost`, `ip6-loopback`, `localhost6`, `metadata.google.internal` and an old-style address that means this server or a metadata address (`0x7f000001`, `0177.0.0.1`, `0x7f.1`, `0xa9fea9fe`, `0x`) with the "always blocked" sentence (any other old-style address, such as `0xc0a80001`, or one with a digit that isn't octal, such as `09.1.1.1`, gets the bad-entry sentence, so write it as `192.168.0.1`), and a fetch refuses those names too.
- **Shopping, Undo of an add that joined a line (0.5 PR 8).** If you add 2 L of milk and it joins the 1 L already on the list, and that line is ticked afterwards (bought, 3 L), Undo of the add now leaves the line alone and says "Nothing to put back: it has all changed again since." It used to lower the amount of a line that was already bought. The same rule already held for Move.
- **Import (0.5 PR 8):** if saving a downloaded photo fails and the clean-up of its files fails too, the request now shows the first error (not the clean-up's), and the log says only the kind of error, never a file's path.
- **A request with half a character in it (0.5 PR 8).** A JSON request containing a lone surrogate (such as `"\ud800"`, half an emoji) now gets a 422 "unprocessable" answer like any other mistake; inside a queued offline change, the change gets a plain refusal instead. Neither is a server error (500) any more. The error answers no longer repeat the text that was sent.
- **The real-recipe check script (0.5 PR 8):** `www.example.com` and `example.com` count as one site, so they share the 5-second gap.

**Before you install it: back up your data.** This version changes the database (the recipes are new tables; nothing existing is rewritten), and the old version can't read a database the new one has changed. Recipe photos are kept in the same data folder, so the backup holds them too. On the server (from `docs/dev/test-on-homelab.md`, section 4.1; replace `<folder>` with the folder that holds your `compose.yaml`):

```bash
cd <folder>
OLD=$(grep -oE 'image: *mealwright:[^ ]+' compose.yaml | cut -d: -f3)
echo "Current version: $OLD"
docker compose stop mealwright
sudo tar -C <folder> --exclude=data/logs -czf "backups/data-$OLD-$(date +%Y%m%d-%H%M).tar.gz" data
sudo chmod 600 backups/data-*.tar.gz
ls -lh backups/       # the new backup should be there, and not tiny
```

- `cd` goes to the Mealwright folder.
- The `OLD=` line reads which version you are running now from `compose.yaml`, and the next line shows it, so the backup's file name says which version it belongs to.
- `docker compose stop mealwright` stops the app, so the database file is copied in one piece.
- The `tar` line makes a dated, compressed copy of the `data` folder in `backups/`, leaving out the log folder.
- `chmod 600` lets only its owner read the backup.
- `ls -lh backups/` shows the new file; it should be there and not tiny.

To go back, follow section 5 of the same file (restore that backup and set the image tag back to 0.3.0).

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-19

The shopping list milestone: shared shopping lists with a Shopping screen, ticking items into the pantry, and a phone that keeps working without a connection (ticks and adds are kept on the phone and sent later). Also live updates, an installable app, and a few small Pantry and Ingredients improvements. Not published; for testing on cabledrawer's homelab.

**Before you install it: back up your data.** This version changes the database (the shopping lists are new tables and columns; nothing existing is rewritten), and the old version can't read a database the new one has changed. On the server (from `docs/dev/test-on-homelab.md`, section 4.1; replace `<folder>` with the folder that holds your `compose.yaml`):


- The `OLD=` line reads which version you are running now from `compose.yaml`, and the next line shows it, so the backup's file name says which version it belongs to.
- `docker compose stop mealwright` stops the app, so the database file is copied in one piece.
- The `tar` line makes a dated, compressed copy of the `data` folder in `backups/`, leaving out the log folder.
- `chmod 600` lets only its owner read the backup.
- `ls -lh backups/` shows the new file; it should be there and not tiny.

### Added

**Shopping**

- **Shopping lists.** Every household has a main list, "This week", that can be renamed but never removed, and any number of extra lists that can be made, renamed and removed, with Undo. A line is an ingredient with an amount; adding an ingredient that is already on the list says so instead of adding it twice. Changes to a list can be undone for 7 days.
- **The Shopping screen.** The list is split by shop, then by aisle, with ticked items folded into "In the trolley · N" at the end of each shop and "Clear ticked" (with Undo). Ingredients with no usual shop go under "Any shop". Tap an amount to change it or take the line off the list. The add box finds ingredients by any of their names. Each ingredient now has an aisle; ingredients you already had get a starting aisle from the bundled table.
- **Typing a name that isn't an ingredient yet** offers "+ New ingredient", which asks **"How do you count it?"** (By weight, By volume, One by one, or Don't count it for a staple), with the likely answer picked first and its packet sizes asked up front. The same question comes from a new **+ New ingredient** button on Ingredients. **+ Staple** is now on the phone's Pantry too: a name nobody has used becomes a new staple, with nothing recorded in the pantry. The guess comes from the bundled table on your server (`GET /api/v1/foods/guess`); nothing is looked up on the internet.
- **Ticking puts the shopping into the pantry.** A tick files what you bought in the ingredient's usual place (Cupboard if it has none), with no use-by date. On the phone a toast says where it went, with **Change** (another place) and Undo; on desktop a **Put away** panel beside the list does the same. Unticking, or Undo, takes it back out and never takes the pantry below zero. **Different pack?** changes what the pantry got ("What did you buy?"), and an item with no amount offers **How much?** instead. Staples and items with no amount put nothing in the pantry. Every tick shows in Pantry's Recent changes ("Bought 2 packets Toor dal → Cupboard") with Undo.
- **Two phones, one list.** Nothing is ever refused with a pop-up because someone else changed the list: the server applies changes in the order it receives them, and where it matters it leaves a **quiet note** under the item, with Undo and a dismiss button, such as "Wren had removed this. Your tick still went into the pantry.", "Wren added milk too" or "Wren changed Milk to 2 L" ("Your other phone" when it was you). Nine kinds of clash have a note (N1 to N9 in `docs/dev/sync-protocol.md`).
- **"Did you buy 2?"** When two phones tick the same item while offline, a card asks, with **Yes, 2** and **Just 1**; the first answer settles it for everyone, and Undo brings the card back. Just 1 takes the extra packet back out of the pantry.
- **Sync for phones** (`POST /api/v1/shopping/actions` and `GET /api/v1/shopping/changes?since=N`): a phone sends a batch of its changes, each with its own ID so that sending one twice does nothing the second time, and gets back an answer for each and everything that changed since it last looked. The batch is described in `docs/dev/sync-protocol.md`, and both are in the OpenAPI document.

**Offline**

- **It works without a connection.** Ticks, adds and every other change to a list are kept on the phone, shown at once, and sent when the connection is back: one batch at a time, in order, tried again after a pause if the server can't be reached. Closing the app doesn't lose them. (iPhone Safari can clear a web app's saved data after about 7 days without use; adding Mealwright to the Home Screen helps.) While offline the bar says "Offline — 3 changes waiting" (on desktop "3 changes waiting. They'll save when you're back."), Shopping's subtitle says "12 to buy · offline, saving on this phone", and when everything is sent a toast says "Back online — all saved".
- **Opening the app offline.** The app opens as the person last signed in on that phone, and Shopping works from the phone's copy, including finding ingredients in the add box. Pantry, Ingredients and Settings need your server: offline they say "You're offline. Pantry needs your Mealwright server — your shopping list works offline." with a Shopping button. Settings still has Sign out and the theme switch, and "+ Add to pantry" says it needs the server. A phone that has never loaded the list says "Your shopping list isn't on this phone yet. It'll be here after you've opened it once while connected."
- **Signing in running out doesn't lose changes.** If your sign-in runs out while offline, the changes stay on the phone: "Please sign in again. Your 3 changes are kept on this phone." Signing back in as the same person sends them at once. A different person is warned first ("Wren's 3 changes haven't been sent yet. If you carry on, they're lost.", with "Sign in as Wren instead" and "Carry on and lose them") and nothing is ever sent under their login.
- **A different login always starts clean.** When someone else signs in on a phone, its offline copy is wiped, as at signing out, and the next start makes a new device ID. A wrong password wipes nothing.
- **Signing out sends first.** Sign out tries to send what is waiting. If something is still unsent: "3 changes haven't been sent yet. If you sign out now, they're lost." with Try sending now, Stay signed in and Sign out and lose them. Signing out wipes the phone's offline copy (the offline database, saved lists and ingredient names, and everything the app kept for the household in the browser), and other tabs of the app clear themselves and go to the sign-in page.
- **A storage error is never "nothing waiting".** If the browser can't read the app's offline store, nothing is deleted or written on that result. The same login carries on (cabledrawer 18 Oct): it never wipes anything. Anyone else pressing Sign in is told "This phone couldn't check for changes waiting to be sent. Nothing has been deleted.", with Try again and "Carry on anyway — anything waiting on this phone may be lost". Settings says the same instead of signing out, with Try again, Stay signed in and "Sign out anyway — anything waiting on this phone may be lost" (cabledrawer 18 Oct). Both "anyway" choices wipe as usual.
- **Signing out loses only the changes the warning showed.** If another tab queues more after "3 changes haven't been sent yet" appears, "Sign out and lose them" warns again with the new count instead of losing them. Adding a change and wiping take turns across tabs (the browser's Web Locks), so a change added at that moment is never lost unseen.
- **A phone that can't delete its offline copy lets nobody in.** If a wipe can't delete the offline store, it is never opened or used again, the session is still ended, and the sign-in page says "This phone couldn't delete its offline copy of the household. Nobody can sign in on it until that's done. Close other Mealwright tabs or restart the browser, then Try again." with a Try again button (cabledrawer 18 Oct). Until Try again has deleted it, the whole sign-in form is disabled (cabledrawer 18 Oct).
- **Carrying on loses only the changes the warning showed.** The sign-in warning remembers which of Wren's changes it showed. If another tab has queued a different one since, it warns again with the new count instead of losing it.
- **No bouncing when the server won't sign out.** If the server answers the sign-out with an error, the sign-in page stays and says "Couldn't finish signing out on this phone." with Try again, instead of going back and forth between the app and the sign-in page. Nothing is deleted. A Try again the server refuses says it again, read out.
- **The sign-in page says why you were signed out** (cabledrawer 18 Oct): "You were signed out because Wren's 3 changes are waiting on this phone. Sign in as Wren to send them." ("Wren's change … to send it" for one; "this computer" on a computer.)
- **Sign in is busy from the first press.** Sign in shows "Signing in…" and ignores a second press (or Enter) straight away, so a quick second press can't start a second sign-in while the phone is still being checked.
- **"Couldn't finish signing out" has a grey Try again**, the quiet look, on that screen only; every other Try again is the main button.
- **"Still stuck?" under the offline copy message.** Under "This phone couldn't delete its offline copy of the household…" one small line says: "Still stuck? Clear this site's data in your browser's settings." The README has a matching Troubleshooting entry.

**Live updates, installing and connection**

- **Live updates.** A second phone or window sees a tick, an added item or a pantry change within a moment, without reloading. They come from your own Mealwright server only (`GET /api/v1/events`, Server-Sent Events); the stream carries numbers, never names. An open screen doesn't count as activity, so the 12-hour sign-out still happens; a kitchen tablet should use "Remember this phone".
- **Installable app.** A manifest, icons made from the brand icon, and a service worker that keeps only the app's own files, so the app's own files (its code, fonts and icons) load without a connection. The screens still need your server; Shopping works offline from the phone's copy (see Offline above). It never keeps anything from the server (`/api`) and never updates itself mid-use: a new version starts the next time every window has been closed. No Install button yet.
- **Connection bar.** "Offline" with Try now on every page (above the tabs on a phone, at the foot of the sidebar on desktop), a short "Back online" toast, and "12 to buy · offline" on Shopping. A blip under 3 seconds shows nothing. With changes waiting it says "Offline — 3 changes waiting" (see Offline above).

**Pantry and Ingredients**

- **Tap a staple to add it to the shopping list.** On Pantry, a staple chip adds that ingredient to the main shopping list with the toast "Oil added to the shopping list · Undo". A small tick shows on the chip while it is on the list; tapping a ticked chip adds nothing more and says "Oil is already on the shopping list." The tap goes through the same add action and waiting list as Shopping, so a tap made while the connection is failing is kept on the phone and sent once, when the server is back. Screen readers hear "Add Oil to the shopping list" or "Oil, on the shopping list". An "Edit staples" link under the chips opens Ingredients with the Staples filter.
- **"+ List" on running-low rows** (hidden in 0.2 until the shopping list worked) does the same for that ingredient (toast with Undo, tick, "already on the shopping list", works through the waiting list).
- **Recent changes on Ingredients.** New ingredients, Staple on or off, removals, merges and edits, each with Undo for 7 days: on desktop a one-line "Recent changes · 9" bar above the editor ("· 10+" past ten) opens the list in the side column; on the phone a "Recent changes" button opens a sheet. It updates after changes you make here, when stock changes anywhere, and when you come back to the page; an ingredient edited on another phone doesn't appear live yet. Pantry's Recent changes still lists stock changes only.
- **Server:** `GET /api/v1/changes?area=ingredients` lists ingredient changes (and their Undos) only, for your household only. No database change for this.
- **Marks in an opened Pantry row.** An opened row with a single packet shows the ¼ · ½ · ¾ · Full marks, with "About 250 g left · type it instead" under them (the link opens the packet menu at "What's left", with the box ready). In a narrow desktop column the marks go two by two, each at least 44 px wide. If saving a mark fails, its tick goes back. The arrow keys move between the marks.

### Database

- **One migration** (`f509d7a1c609`): six new tables for the shopping lists (`shopping_list`, `list_item`, `list_tick`, `sync_action`, `list_note` and `double_tick`), `aisle` and `version` on ingredients, `tick_id` on changes, and `list_seq` and `list_purged_seq` on the household. It makes each household's main list "This week" and gives existing ingredients a starting aisle from the bundled table. Nothing existing is rewritten. - Old shopping-list notes and answered questions are tidied away after 7 days, and removed lists and lines after 12 months, by the same hourly background job that summarises old pantry history. A phone that was behind a tidy-up gets a fresh copy of the lists; changes still waiting on it are kept and sent after.

### Privacy and security

- **Nothing new leaves your server.** Live updates, the sync endpoints and the app's service worker talk only to your own Mealwright server, and the conversions table and the "How do you count it?" guess stay on it too.
- **The live stream carries numbers, never names.**
- **Every `/api` answer is sent with `Cache-Control: no-store`, and the service worker never keeps anything from `/api`.**
- **What the phone keeps, and when it goes.** The offline copy of the lists lives in the browser's own database (IndexedDB). Signing out, or a different person signing in, wipes it, along with the app's other saved data about the household. A sign-in that merely runs out keeps it on purpose, so the changes waiting aren't lost. Besides the theme and sidebar choices and two flags for an unfinished sign-out or wipe, the only thing put in `localStorage` is the last login's number and username (no password or session), so the app knows whose phone it is when the offline copy can't be read; the wipe removes it too.
- One household can never see or change another's lists, notes, questions or changes (tested on every endpoint). An ID from another household is answered exactly like one that doesn't exist.

### Privacy and security

- **A blocked outside connection is logged in plain words and names only the switch** ("recipe import is switched off"), never the site or address, which could show what someone was cooking. A test checks it. (Asked by cabledrawer.)

Milestone 0.1, Foundation: an empty but real Mealwright you can install, sign in to and click around, with the safety rails built in. Not published; for testing on cabledrawer's homelab.

### Added

- **One Docker image** with the web app and its API. It runs as an unprivileged user (UID 10001), keeps everything in `/data`, has a health check, and contains no `pip`. `compose.example.yaml` shows how to run it behind a reverse proxy.
- **First run:** the first visitor creates the admin account (there's no default admin), then the app opens.
- **Sign in and out**, with "Remember this device" for 30 days. Passwords need at least 10 characters and are stored scrambled (Argon2). After 5 wrong guesses, sign-in slows down, per account and per real address, so one person's guesses don't slow down anyone else.
- **The app shell:** bottom tabs on phone and a collapsible sidebar on desktop, with empty Today, Plan, Shopping, Pantry and Recipes pages and a Settings page with Sign out.
- **Auto · Light · Dark** in plain sight, saved to your account. A designed dark theme, with fonts bundled.
- **One switch component** for every on/off.
- **API** under `/api/v1`, described at `/api/v1/openapi.json`. `GET /api/v1/version` reports the version for update tools such as Diun.
- **Database changes are applied on start-up** (migrations: household, Privacy switches, logins and sessions).
- **`./check`** runs every check before each pull request: lint, types, tests, browser tests, licence allow list, pip-audit, osv-scanner, Bandit, and Trivy plus a no-telemetry check on the image.
- Licence (AGPL-3.0), README, contributing guide, security policy, this changelog, and the homelab testing guide (`docs/dev/test-on-homelab.md`).

### Privacy and security

- **The outbound gate:** the only way Mealwright connects to anything outside your server. Each kind of connection has its own Privacy switch, and **all of them are off**. While the app runs, any other connection or address lookup is refused, whether it comes from our code or a library.
- **No telemetry:** FastAPI's built-in telemetry is switched off, and no telemetry package is installed (checked in the code and in the image).
- **Strict browser rules (Content Security Policy):** pages load files only from your own server. A real-browser test proves an outside script is blocked. Location, camera and microphone are off; referrers aren't sent.
- **Logs** hold only sign-ins and errors, never what people view or search for (the web server's request log is off).
- **HTTPS is required for sign-in.** On a plain `http://` address, Mealwright says so instead of failing silently; `MEALWRIGHT_ALLOW_PLAIN_HTTP` allows it for testing, with a warning.
- **Works behind any reverse proxy.** Its forwarded headers are believed only from `MEALWRIGHT_TRUSTED_PROXIES` (off by default).
- Requests from other sites are refused, and the session cookie can't be read by scripts and is stored only as a fingerprint.
